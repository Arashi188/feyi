Business problem

A multi-region Nigerian business needs one place to see sales, budget, targets and expenses by region, channel and product category.

Data

A practice dataset of 11 linked tables (dates, products, customers, employees, regions, departments, sales, expenses, budget and targets), all in naira. It holds 8,000 sales orders from January 2023 to December 2026, 1,800 expense records and 600 customers.

Process

  • Summarised sales, quantity, budget, targets, expenses and headcount with 12 PivotTables
  • Built 12 charts from those PivotTables
  • Arranged the charts on two dashboard sheets
  • Compared net sales by region, sales channel and product category

Key insights

  • Net sales total ₦9.63bn. Lagos leads the regions at ₦1.68bn and South West trails at ₦1.49bn, about 11% behind.
  • Online is the top channel at ₦2.51bn and Retail the lowest at ₦2.32bn.
  • Office Supplies is the top category at ₦1.80bn, just ahead of Industrial Equipment at ₦1.78bn. Beverages is lowest at ₦1.37bn.

Recommendation and impact

One dashboard that shows where sales come from by region, channel and category, next to total expenses of ₦2.77bn, budgets and targets.

Business problem

Sales data collected by hand is full of duplicates, blanks and inconsistent labels, so its totals cannot be trusted until it is cleaned.

Data

A practice file of 1,015 orders dated from 2025, with 16 columns. Problems found: 14 duplicate rows, blanks in seven columns (up to 209 in Customer_Gender and 181 in Payment_Method), 5 orders with zero or negative quantity, discounts below 0% or above 100%, 3 dates in the future, and inconsistent spellings in Payment_Method (7 variants for 5 methods) and Order_Status (4 for 3).

  • It tracks month-over-month growth and year-to-date sales.
  • It breaks sales down by salesperson, order status, payment method and customer gender.

Recommendation and impact

A cleaned dataset behind a report that answers where sales and profit come from and how they change month to month.

Business problem

Household chores quietly use a large part of the week, and it is hard to see which ones take the most time or how to organise them to save time.

Data

A MySQL database with a tasks table (8 chores with category, weekly frequency, estimated minutes and whether they can be batched) and a task_history table that logs actual minutes and completion, linked by task_id.

Process

  • Designed the two tables and loaded the chore and history data in SQL
  • Calculated weekly minutes per chore and per category with GROUP BY, and compared estimated and actual minutes with JOINs
  • Pulled the data into pandas, ranked chores by weekly duration and validated it for missing values, duplicate IDs and invalid frequencies
  • Built a workload-balanced weekly schedule and a batching recommendation, then exported the results to Excel

Key insights

  • The workload is 685 minutes (11.4 hours) a week across 8 chores.
  • Cleaning accounts for 565 of those minutes, about 82%.
  • Sweep and mop is the biggest single chore at 210 minutes a week, followed by laundry at 160.
  • If batching saves 15% on batchable chores, about 103 minutes (1.7 hours) a week could be saved.

Recommendation and impact

Recommendation: group chores of the same category into single sessions and spread the load evenly across the week. The 15% saving is an assumption in the notebook, not a measured result.

Business problem

A clinic needs to know which patient is seeing which doctor and when, without repeating patient and doctor details on every appointment.

Data

Three tables created in MySQL: patients (name, age, gender, phone, consultation fee, medication fee and total bill), doctors (name, age and specialization) and appointments (patient, doctor and date). Sample records were added for testing.

Process

  • Defined primary keys and made the appointments table reference patients and doctors with foreign keys
  • Inserted sample records to test the design
  • Wrote lookups by ID, exact name and partial name with LIKE
  • Joined all three tables to list each appointment with the patient, doctor, specialization and date

Key insights

  • Storing each patient and doctor once keeps appointment records small and consistent.
  • A single JOIN query returns a readable appointment list from three tables.

Recommendation and impact

A working schema that can be extended with more records, billing queries and reports.

Business problem

Practising analysis needs realistic raw data, and a ready-made dataset is not always available.

Data

Synthetic data: 500 employee sales records for 2024 to 2026 across five departments, four regions and five products. Units sold, unit price and targets are random, and revenue is calculated as units × price.

Process

  • Defined the ten fields in a dictionary, with lists of departments, regions and products
  • Looped 500 times to create random records and calculate revenue
  • Built a pandas DataFrame, checked the first and last rows and saved it to CSV

Key insights

  • Revenue equals units × price in every row by construction, which makes the data easy to use for testing validation checks.
  • The data is synthetic, so it is for practising technique rather than drawing business conclusions.

Recommendation and impact

A reusable generator that produces fresh practice data for sales analysis exercises.
