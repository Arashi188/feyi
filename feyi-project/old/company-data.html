Sales Analytics

Company Sales Performance: 500 Transactions

Python · Pandas · Matplotlib · Business Analysis

The Business Problem

A mid-sized company provided 500 raw sales records spanning 2024–2026 across multiple departments, regions, and products. Leadership needed clarity on three questions:

  • Which regions and products are actually driving revenue?
  • Where are we missing targets by the largest margin?
  • Where should the sales team focus their effort next quarter?

The dataset was intentionally messy: inconsistent currency formatting, missing target values, and no pre-calculated performance metrics. The goal was not just to visualize the data, but to produce a decision-ready recommendation.

My Approach

1. Data Cleaning & Validation

I loaded the CSV into Pandas and performed an initial audit. Key cleaning steps included:

  • Standardizing column names (e.g., units_price → Unit_Price)
  • Verifying Revenue = Units_Sold × Unit_Price to catch calculation errors
  • Handling missing Target values by flagging them for review
  • Ensuring all years fell within the expected 2024–2026 range

2. Exploratory Analysis

I grouped performance by Region and Product, calculating:

  • Total revenue per region
  • Average target attainment rate (Revenue / Target)
  • Top and bottom performing product categories
  • Year-over-year revenue trends

3. Gap Analysis

I created a Target_Gap column (Revenue − Target) to identify which departments were furthest from their goals. This surfaced a pattern: Finance and Procurement consistently underperformed relative to Sales and IT, despite similar unit volumes.

Key Findings

North

Highest total revenue region, but also the highest target variance.

Product D

Top revenue driver, but margins varied significantly by region.

Finance

Largest average gap between actual revenue and target.

The analysis revealed that targets were not evenly calibrated across regions. North and West regions received disproportionately high targets relative to their historical unit volumes, creating a structural disadvantage for those teams.

Business Recommendation

Based on the findings, I recommended a three-part action plan:

  1. Re-baseline targets by region. Use the 2024–2025 average revenue as the baseline for 2026 targets, rather than applying uniform percentage increases.
  2. Investigate Finance's performance gap. The data suggests a possible product mix issue—Finance sold disproportionately more low-price items. A targeted product training session could address this.
  3. Double down on North region. Despite missing targets, North generated the most revenue. Additional sales headcount there has the highest expected ROI.
